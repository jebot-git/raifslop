# Rods, panorama detail and environmental movement

The four tackle tiers now use Blender-authored generic spinning rods: continuous tapered graphite blanks, diminishing ceramic line guides, thread bindings, metal reel seats, cork or EVA grips, gear housings, wound spools, drag caps and bail wires. The crank has a separate rotating mesh. Construction references: [Shimano Sienna spinning rods](https://fish.shimano.com/en-GB/product/rods/freshwaterpredator/versatilespinning/p-sienna-spinning.html) and [spinning reel assembly schematic](https://fish.shimano.com/content/dam/Shimanofish/LocalNZ/Productrelatedpage/reels/schematics/EV_EN_OVS_51SF24M062X_20STLSW60HG.pdf). No branding or CAD was copied. Source: `source/rods.blend`, rebuilt by `tools/build_rods.py`. [Rendered comparison](rods.png).

Existing 1.68 m casting-tip and tracked-reeling anchors are preserved. Rod tier and crank angle are validated and replicated to remote anglers; local purchase rules remain unchanged. Rods are static blanks, not physically simulated bending rods.

All four playable panoramas now use native 4096×2048 HDR photographs, replacing 2048×1024 runtime copies. The three pier sources were already retained at 4K; [Lakeside](https://polyhaven.com/a/lakeside) was reacquired at 4K from Poly Haven (CC0). No image was AI-upscaled. Only the active panorama is loaded without resource caching. Current imported texture payload is approximately 32 MiB per active sky. Soft lighting/exposure and the 128-pixel reflection radiance map are preserved; radiance resolution affects reflections, not the visible panorama.

Location-specific birds alternate flapping and gliding. Shore insects are sparse: two small grey flies at Lakeside (12 mm wingspan, 7 mm body), three midges at Gray Pier and two dragonflies at Bell Park Pier; the harbour and coastal locations have none. Flies use one wing pair and tight, quick flight paths instead of broad butterfly wings and slow fluttering. They use two MultiMeshes, a shared wing-animation shader, bounded trajectories and no dynamic lights, shadows, collision or network traffic. Location changes replace the complete wildlife group with the foreground. These are cosmetic authored additions, not observations from the photographed locations. Distant traffic was not added because the current gameplay-facing water views do not provide a reliable 3D road surface to attach it to.

## Stereo and Gaussian splatting assessment

The resumed [Gaussian splatting assessment](GAUSSIAN_SPLATTING.md) compares current renderers, audits gdgs camera sorting at a pinned revision, and defines the content and device acceptance gates. Both shortlisted paths need explicit handling for the simultaneous Guide/selfie camera.

[Godot PanoramaSkyMaterial](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_panoramaskymaterial.html) samples an equirectangular image. The supplied photographs are mono. Higher source resolution improves detail but cannot reveal surfaces hidden behind foreground objects or reconstruct a second viewpoint. Offsetting that same panorama separately for each eye would invent depth and would not support head translation. Current modeled terrain, rods, fish and wildlife already have correct binocular rendering and positional parallax.

A captured stereo/omnidirectional stereo panorama could provide disparity around its capture origin, but would still need carefully bounded locomotion. A depth-reconstructed shell would require credible depth and disocclusion filling; a single image does not provide those reliably. Neither synthetic stereo conversion nor an unverified depth shell is enabled in these builds.

Gaussian splats are a viable future scene source when a multi-view scan and redistribution rights are available. [godot-gsplat](https://github.com/shiena/godot-gsplat) provides a Node3D-based path, XR presets and VR multiview handling. Its documented limitation is one sorting order for multiple simultaneous cameras, which conflicts with the Guide camera sharing the game world. Integration would need an isolated camera world or renderer changes, opaque-water/foreground occlusion checks, and Quest/Pico frame-time and memory measurements. [A Godot Quest 3 splat viewer](https://github.com/Takio0304/godot-quest3-gs-viewer) demonstrates another available starting point. No splat renderer or unverified scan is bundled in this release.

The next panorama tier could be native 8K, increasing linear detail by another factor of two but texture storage by four (about 128 MiB before any mipmaps at the current import format). A selectable desktop-only high-resolution pack is preferable to imposing that allocation on standalone headsets. Actual Quest/Pico performance is still a hardware acceptance test; the present release chooses native 4K for every platform.
