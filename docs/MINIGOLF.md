# Waterfront minigolf

`main` follows the EOS branch. The full-size Golf Minus integration is archived; its courses, terrain, club bag, drivers, irons and full-swing physics are removed. Shared avatars, identity, voice, fishing, BBQ, controller calibration and score services remain.

Open **Menu → Minigolf → Join minigolf at this water**. The course occupies connected waterfront decks in the same location. Joining never changes the player's network water ID. Other players can fish, cook, talk and see golfers without enrolling. In multiplayer, participants join the water's shared round; its owner presses **Start shared round**. A round supports one to eight golfers. Offline play starts immediately and resumes a saved card.

Each course has 18 different shot puzzles with par, fixed tees and cups, rail banks, obstacles, slopes and shallow-water hazards. Layouts are deterministic JSON generated by `tools/build_minigolf_courses.py`. The themes use different obstacle sequences, dimensions, slopes and bank angles. Every hole has a hidden collectible ball.

## Play and fitting

- Hold the striking-hand **grip** to enable contact. Release to practice. Trigger teleports beside your stationary ball; A/X collects a nearby hidden ball; B/Y opens the shared menu. Hand tracking uses the existing optical grip gesture.
- The ball rolls with slope acceleration and friction. Swept putter contact and fixed physics substeps reject tracking jumps and prevent tunnelling. Fast putts can cross the cup; sufficiently slow entries drop. Water returns the ball to its previous lie and adds one stroke. The limit is 12 strokes per hole.
- **Fit controller angle and putter length** retains the original stable-address solver. Hold your address pose, press trigger to capture, then A/X to accept or B/Y to cancel. Preview does not change the accepted profile; Undo restores the previous fit. Manual per-hand controller, shaft and face angles, position offsets and physical-mount mode remain available.
- Accepted values are read from the existing `user://golf_controls.cfg`. Fishing controller calibration remains independent. Left-handed play and optional manual teleport-to-ball are supported. Hole changes, settled shots and fitting never reposition the player; manual teleport preserves facing. The former visual support-hand snap is retained, including a controller set down or untracked, while putter physics continues sampling the striking hand.
- Pick up the existing-style guide at the opposite hip. Its three pages show the current hole map/par, your 18-hole scorecard, and participating players' scores. Thumbstick or physical page buttons change pages. The shared menu can also open it. The guide camera and selfie controls remain available.
- Visiting BBQ makes a golfer temporarily absent and hides/disarms the putter. Fishing and BBQ users have no minigolf turn restrictions. Returning restores participation; leaving minigolf restores the original fishing pose.

The controls and short-course play draw on the public [Walkabout resources](https://www.mightycoconut.com/wmg-resources) and [grip-to-putt description](https://www.mightycoconut.com/gtp). Course layouts, art and sound are this project's own. Desktop keyboard controls exist only for non-XR testing; production startup still requires VR.

## Persistence and networking

Protocol **21** is required on all clients/servers. Water identities stay shared. Existing pose fields carry a putter-only marker, shaft transform/length and independent fitted face transform. Server-owned turns and stroke grants reuse the existing ENet/EOS service. Only enrolled golfers appear on the guide's roster. Scores from archived full-size courses remain readable in saved records but are excluded from active course selection/rankings. Retired online outbox entries are preserved separately while fishing/minigolf synchronization continues.

Offline cards live in `user://minigolf_<water>.cfg`; preferences and collectible IDs live in `user://minigolf.cfg`. New online board and destination definitions are provided in `EOS_LEADERBOARDS.example.json` and `DESTINATIONS.example.json`. Provider portals must provision these new definitions before their cloud boards can accept scores; no portal changes are made by this source update.

## Validation

`python3 tools/test_minigolf.py` runs isolated gameplay, fitting, runtime, pose, ranking, online-outbox, achievement, destination and fishing regressions. `python3 tools/test_minigolf_network.py` runs an actual loopback server and two clients to verify a golfer can finish a round while an unenrolled angler runs BBQ at the same water. The fixture respects the server command rate limit.

Godot MCP can run `tools/minigolf_preview.tscn` for visual inspection. `tools/minigolf_preview.gd` renders a named course to `test-results/minigolf-<water>.png`. Blender sources and mesh/material credits are in `source/minigolf/` and `assets/minigolf/MATERIAL_CREDITS.md`.

These automated checks and rendered previews do not substitute for headset comfort/putter-feel testing or Quest performance measurements. No production EOS lobby or leaderboard was exercised by these tests.

## Course design revision 2

The existing deck footprints now contain 18 mechanically distinct designs per water: opening bank, dogleg, twin gates, crossfall, crest, split decision, causeway, switchback, sunken garden, double cushion, keyhole, saddle, island hopping, needle ridge, terraces, ricochet alley, volcano and final approach. Three difficulty bands introduce individual skills before combining them. Water-specific mirroring, dimensions, ordering and elevation vary the shots while retaining themed scenery.

Reference study (29 September 2026):

- [Myminigolf obstacle catalogue](https://www.myminigolf.com/myminigolf-1?lang=en): wave/bridge pace control, gate accuracy, elevated targets and successive banked curves inspired the shot families.
- [Mighty Coconut's Myst course](https://www.mightycoconut.com/myst-dlc): treating a hole as an individual puzzle informed the distinct routing problems.
- [US ProMiniGolf Association rulebook](https://www.prominigolf.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/Members-Rulebook1.pdf): functional, predictable and mechanically different obstacles informed readable banks and clear penalty boundaries. These are original adventure courses, not tournament-certified reproductions.

Raised plateaus reach about 0.7 m; recessed gardens descend about 0.5 m. Smooth ramps and elliptical mounds share exact analytical height/gradient functions between physics and rendering. Banks follow the terrain, deck subfloors clear the recesses, and water cutouts match hazard boundaries. Level tees/cup landings prevent runaway resting balls. Guide maps show slope arrows and a hole-specific tactical hint. This revision uses static geometry and terrain rather than timed moving machinery.

Course JSON version 2 prevents resuming a version-1 lie inside new geometry: an unfinished older card starts a new round. Saved fitting profiles and collectibles remain intact. Protocol 21 prevents clients with the previous geometry joining the same session. Existing historical score records are retained; their layout versions were not recorded and they are not re-rated.

`tests/minigolf_terrain.gd` checks analytical gradients against numerical derivatives across all 216 holes, level tees/cups, elevation bounds, design coverage, and underhit versus sufficient-pace ramp behavior. `tests/test_minigolf_layouts.py` independently searches dry routes through every hole. These checks establish geometry/physics consistency; human headset playtesting is still needed to tune pars and subjective difficulty.

## Location art and previews

Each water has an explicit decoration palette in `scripts/minigolf/decoration.gd`, with locally appropriate obstacle dressing and narrow planted beds outside the walking strips and playing lanes. Harbour props use iron/rope/mooring motifs; coastal props use granite, pale driftwood, shells and dune grass; river props use grasses, mossy cedar, ferns and alpine snow patches. Course names are mounted on timber signboards. A small material fill keeps overcast greens, banks and props readable without adding dynamic lights. Decorative changes do not modify ball collision, course layout versions, pars or putting routes.

`tools/render_minigolf_gallery.gd` renders all twelve locations as overview/detail pairs in `test-results/minigolf-gallery/after/`. Run it in graphical Godot with XR off, then run `python3 tools/build_minigolf_gallery.py` to create the searchable local `index.html`. The gallery uses the production panorama material, sky-based ambient lighting, measured location sun, yaw and filmic tone mapping; its water plane is simplified and it includes the modeled fishing shoreline. Preview panoramas are resized to 4K to bound renderer memory. `--before` writes to a separate baseline folder. The baseline made during this revision used the earlier preview ambient/tone-mapping setup and is not a lighting-controlled comparison.

Blender geometry sources, Krita `.kra` textures and attribution are retained alongside the generated assets. The existing gameplay regression suite passes with the decorations installed; headset performance must still be measured on device.

### Shore connections and simulated sessions

Lakeside, Lake Pier, Gray Pier and Bell Park have moored shuttle boats, beside the middle gallery over water. The native reverse-view audit showed that Lake Pier’s distant photographic apron is not an actual quay connection. Coastal and river courses remain on solid ground. `tools/audit_minigolf_ground.gd` samples the actual authored meshes. The river bank continues beneath the rear course rows, which extend past the former foreground boundary.

`tests/minigolf_transitions_xr.gd` exercises a native Monado simulated HMD with synthetic controllers: fishing, BBQ, minigolf, manual ball teleport, fitting, hole advance, missing-offhand snapping and return to fishing. Stereo captures are in `test-results/minigolf-vr/`. All assertions passed. Native OpenXR teardown still reports session-stopping/spatial-signal and interaction-profile allocation warnings; this is not a human comfort or Quest performance test.

`tools/test_minigolf_network.py` runs a dedicated server and three distinct golfer, angler and cook clients. `tools/test_minigolf_three_activities.py` runs three rendered full game scenes and saves each player's view. The latter uses reduced panorama textures to fit the test workstation's memory. Both verify shared location identity and independent activity states. The dedicated-server records suite also covers persistence, duplicate rejection and reconnect identity.

### Reverse-view panorama audit

`tools/test_minigolf_pan.py` captures a native Monado stereo half-turn at the rear gallery, at 15° intervals. The rig remains at `(0, 2, 64)` while yaw changes; the simulated headset retains its small tracking motion. `test-results/minigolf-vr-pan/index.html` shows both eyes with a yaw slider. Captures read the HDR buffer before display tonemapping and can appear darker than the final headset image.

This inspection exposed a rectangular river-bank extension and stretched photographic ground behind coastal courses. River extensions now share the existing bank material, including Glacier Run’s snow, meet its exact boundary and blend their distant edges into the panorama. Coastal course surroundings retain real textured terrain before the distant photographic transition. The source panorama remains a fixed distant image; close trees/buildings represented only in that photograph cannot provide true positional parallax.

## Baked lighting and texture filtering

All twelve courses have Blender Cycles direct/indirect irradiance and ambient-occlusion bakes, with packed UV2 geometry validated against the runtime mesh signatures. The retained scenes live in `source/minigolf/lighting`; `tools/export_minigolf_bake.gd`, `tools/unwrap_minigolf_lightmaps.py`, `tools/bake_minigolf_lighting.py` and `tools/compile_minigolf_lightmaps.gd` reproduce the pipeline. Unwrapping requires NumPy and xatlas. Existing measured location lighting is reused; Cedar Creek and Glacier Run use their HDR environments. Maps are 2048² and mipmapped. Export preserves lossless HDR lighting data on desktop and Quest.

Thin foliage remains dynamically lit because its subpixel atlas islands cannot retain stable samples; it still contributes shadows to the static bake. Balls, players and putters also retain live lighting. `tests/minigolf_lightmaps.gd` checks all twelve geometry signatures, UV2 coverage and map mipmaps. `tools/audit_texture_mipmaps.gd` audits imported game textures and embedded model textures. Procedural turf and timber detail fades with pixel footprint to avoid distant shimmer.
